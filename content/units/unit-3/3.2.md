# 3.2 — Connect Percent and Proportion

Source: Lesson 3-2 student pages (pp. 135–140), 3-2 Additional Practice (pp. 33–34) · Built: 2026-11-09

## In one sentence
Draw a bar diagram with the whole as 100% and the part as P%, then solve the percent proportion part/whole = P/100 for whichever of the three pieces is missing.

## Key concepts and vocabulary
| Term | Plain meaning | Tiny example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Percent proportion | Part over whole equals P over 100 | 18/25 = P/100 |
| Bar diagram | Two bars: the whole is 100%, the part is P% | a long bar marked 100% above a shorter bar marked P% |
| Find the part | Whole and percent are given; use part/whole = P/100 | 40% of 70 → n/70 = 40/100 |
| Find the percent | Part and whole are given; solve for P | 18 out of 25 → 18/25 = P/100 |
| Find the whole | Part and percent are given; solve for the whole | 42 is 60% of w → 42/w = 60/100 |
| Cross-multiplying | In a proportion, multiply across both ways | a/b = c/d gives a × d = b × c |
| Reasonable answer | Does the result fit with 100%? | if P is over 100, the part is bigger than the whole |

**Big idea:** every percent problem is a proportion with 100 on the bottom of one ratio. Decide which of the three pieces (part, whole, percent) is missing, write part/whole = P/100, and solve.

## Problem profile
| Type | What's given → what's asked | Number ranges / forms | Format | Share of sheet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Find the percent | part + whole → percent | counts such as 9 of 12, 154 of 350, 13 of 22; money such as a down payment out of a price | fill in the proportion, typed answer | ~25% |
| B. Find the part | whole + percent → part | percent 2..175; whole 3 .. 250, decimals like 12.5 | word problem, bar diagram | ~20% |
| C. Find the whole | part + percent → whole | percent 4..200; part a decimal such as 8,219.5 | word problem | ~20% |
| D. Read a picture first | a pie chart, label or receipt → pick the right numbers, then find a percent | counts in a pie chart, a nutrition label percent | chart + typed answer | ~10% |
| E. Compare with percents | two sizes or prices of the same thing → "better buy?" or "which group is bigger?" | prices to the cent, groups of different sizes | explain | ~10% |
| F. Money, multi-part | tax and tip together; total cost is a percent of the original; sale price and profit | 120%, 140%, 180%; rates 4%..20% | part a, part b; multiple choice with 4 options | ~15% |
| G. Name it and estimate | "is this asking for the percent, the part, or the whole?"; a quick estimate such as 380% of 60 | | short answer, explain | a few |

**Difficulty ladder:** find the percent from small counts → find the part with a whole-number percent → find the part with a percent over 100 → find the whole → read a chart first → two-part money problems → explain why an answer must be bigger or smaller than another number.

## Methods: ways to attack it

### Method 1: "Bar diagram, then proportion"
1. Draw two bars. Label the longer one 100% (the whole) and the other P% (the part). If the percent is over 100, the part bar is the longer one.
2. Put the numbers you know on the bars and a letter on the one you do not.
3. Write part/whole = P/100.
4. Solve: multiply both sides by the number under the letter, or cross-multiply.
**Memory hook:** "Part over whole equals P over 100."
**Check it:** does the answer fit the diagram? Is the part smaller than the whole when P is under 100?

### Method 2: "Percent to decimal"
If the part is the unknown, multiply the whole by the decimal form of the percent (as in 3.1). Use the proportion when the percent or the whole is the unknown.

### Method 3: "Benchmark check"
Find 10% and 50% of the whole, and see whether your answer lands near them.

## Guiding questions (for homework help)
1. "Which number is the whole? Which is the part?"
2. "Draw two bars. What goes on the top bar? What goes on the bottom?"
3. "What is the unknown: the part, the whole, or the percent?"
4. "Can you write part over whole equals P over 100?"
5. "Does your answer make sense next to 100%?"

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | What it looks like | Nudge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Puts the numbers in the wrong spots | whole on top of the first ratio | "Part goes on top of whole. Which number is bigger here?" |
| Forgets the 100 | writes part/whole = P | "Percent always sits over 100." |
| Solves for the wrong thing | gives the part when the whole was asked | "What is the letter standing for?" |
| Answer not checked | a part larger than the whole when P is under 100% | "Is your part smaller than the whole?" |
| Mixing up the tip and the tax | adds both to the plain price in the wrong order | "Is the tip based on the price, or on the price with tax?" |
| Uses the wrong chart numbers | uses 'percent' labels as counts, or the wrong slice | "Read the label: is that a count or a percent?" |

## Variant recipes
- **A:** `a of b` with b in {8, 12, 20, 25, 40, 50, 250, 350} and a < b; ask for the percent; some money versions (down payment out of a price).
- **B:** `p% of W` with p in {2, 15, 35, 60, 150, 175, 200} and W in 3..250 with a decimal sometimes (12.5, 3.2).
- **C:** `N is p% of what?` with p in {20, 60, 85, 120, 200} and N chosen so the whole comes out clean.
- **D:** a pie chart with three slices of counts; ask for one slice's percent. A label with percent and amount: find the daily total.
- **E:** two sizes with two prices; convert each to a percent (or a unit price) and say which is the better buy.
- **F:** tax t% and tip s% on a meal; or an item sold for p% of what the store paid, then profit.
- **G:** classify the unknown in a sentence; estimate `p% of N` with p over 100 using rounding.

## Worked example (an original problem, not from the sheet)
*A class has 25 students and 18 brought lunch from home. What percent brought lunch?*
1. Draw two bars. The whole bar is 25 students (100%). The part bar is 18 students (P%).
2. Write 18/25 = P/100.
3. Multiply both sides by 100: P = 18 × 100 ÷ 25 = 72.
72% of the class brought lunch. Check: 18 is a little less than 25, so a number a bit under 100% makes sense (25% is 6, and 18 is three of those, 75%).
*Find the whole:* 42 is 60% of what? 42/w = 60/100, so w = 42 × 100 ÷ 60 = 70.
