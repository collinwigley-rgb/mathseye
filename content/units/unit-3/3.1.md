# 3.1 — Understand Percent as a Proportion

Source: Lesson 3-1 student pages (pp. 129–134), 3-1 Additional Practice (pp. 31–32; titled "Analyze Percents of Numbers") · Built: 2026-11-09

## In one sentence
A percent is a fraction out of 100, so you can find the part of any amount by matching P/100 to part/whole, and you can fairly compare parts of different-sized groups.

## Key concepts and vocabulary
| Term | Plain meaning | Tiny example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Percent | Out of 100: P percent means P/100 | 35% = 35/100 = 0.35 |
| Part | The piece you are finding or comparing | 12 out of 40: the part is 12 |
| Whole | The full amount the percent is "of" | 12 out of 40: the whole is 40 |
| Percent of a number | Whole × the percent written as a decimal | 25% of 48 = 0.25 × 48 = 12 |
| Commission | Pay that is a percent of what someone sells | 4% of $500 in sales = $20 |
| Sales tax | A percent of the price, added at checkout | 6% of $30 = $1.80 more |
| Tip | A percent of the bill, left for the server | 15% of $20 = $3 |
| More than 100% | A part that is bigger than the whole | 150% of 20 = 30 |

**Big idea:** percent means "per 100," so P% of a number is the number times P/100. That lets you compare parts of different-sized wholes fairly, and any percent over 100% means the answer is bigger than the whole.

## Methods: ways to attack it

### Method 1: "Percent to decimal, then multiply"
1. What is the whole (the number after "of")? What is the percent?
2. Write the percent as a decimal: move the point two places left (35% → 0.35, 4% → 0.04, 0.8% → 0.008).
3. Multiply the whole by that decimal.
**Memory hook:** "Percent means per 100, so slide the point two places left."
**Check it:** is the answer smaller than the whole when the percent is under 100%, and bigger when it is over 100%?

### Method 2: "Percent proportion"
Write part/whole = P/100 and solve for the unknown. This is the same idea as an equivalent-ratio table from Unit 2: the second ratio always has 100 on the bottom.

### Method 3: "Benchmark percents"
10% is one-tenth (divide by 10). 1% is one-hundredth (divide by 100). 50% is half. 25% is a quarter. Build other percents from these (15% = 10% + 5%, 35% = 25% + 10%).
**Check it:** use a benchmark to see whether your exact answer is close.

### Method 4: "Over 100% means more than the whole"
100% is the whole, 200% is double, 300% is triple. For 125%, find the whole and add a quarter more.

## Guiding questions (for homework help)
1. "What is the whole here? What is the part, or is the part what we're finding?"
2. "Is the percent under 100% or over it? So should the answer be smaller or bigger than the whole?"
3. "How do you write that percent as a decimal?"
4. "Can you check it with 10% or 50%?"
5. For tax, tip and commission: "Is the question asking for just the extra amount, or the total with it added?"

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | What it looks like | Nudge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moves the point the wrong way or only once | 4% written as 0.4, or 35% as 3.5 | "4 out of 100: is that 0.4 or 0.04?" |
| Tiny percents | 1/4 % written as 0.25 instead of 0.0025 | "0.25% still needs the point moved two places" |
| Stops at the tax | gives the tax when asked for the total cost | "What does the question want: the extra, or the total?" |
| Treats 125% as 25% | answers with a number smaller than the whole | "Is 125% more or less than the whole?" |
| Reverses a percent | "125% of A means A is 75% of the result" | "Try real numbers: A = 100, then 125. What percent of 125 is 100?" |
| Mixes up part and whole | divides the whole by the part | "Which number comes after 'of'?" |
| Percents of different wholes | thinks 40% of a small group is more than 25% of a big group | "Work out the actual counts first." |

## Variant recipes
- **A:** `p% of W` with p in {5, 8, 12, 15, 20, 35, 40, 75, 80} and W a multiple of 5 up to 800, or money W up to $7,500; add a few tiny p in {0.5, 0.75, 0.8}.
- **B:** `p% as much as W` with p in {110, 120, 125, 130, 150, 200, 320} and W in 4..60; ask for the new amount.
- **C:** price or sales W, rate r in 3..8 (%); part a = the extra (tax, tip or commission), part b = total, what the store keeps, or profit.
- **D:** `N is p% of what number?` with p small (0.5..5) and N chosen so the whole is a whole number.
- **E:** a percent with a fraction (1/2 %, 1/4 %, 3/500 %) → decimal or fraction; and a "what was the error?" version.
- **F:** `Is p% of N less than, between, or greater than a and b?` with p over 100; and a true-or-false reversing claim with real numbers.
- **G:** percent of a percent (p of q of W); fill-level before and after (a% full to b% full of a capacity); hourly wage versus commission over a week.

## Worked example (an original problem, not from the sheet)
*A theater has 240 seats and 35% of them are in the balcony. How many seats are in the balcony?*
1. The whole is 240 and the percent is 35%.
2. 35% = 0.35, and 0.35 × 240 = 84.
3. Check with benchmarks: 10% of 240 is 24, so 30% is 72, and 5% is 12, so 35% is 72 + 12 = 84. ✓
There are 84 balcony seats.
