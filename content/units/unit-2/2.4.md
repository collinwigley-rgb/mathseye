# 2.4 — Describe Proportional Relationships: Constant of Proportionality

Source: Lesson 2-4 student pages, 2-4 Additional Practice (pp. 23–24) · Built: 2026-11-03

## In one sentence
The constant of proportionality **k = y/x** is the unit rate, and every proportional relationship can be written as **y = kx**.

## Key concepts and vocabulary
| Term | Plain meaning | Tiny example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constant of proportionality (k) | The fixed multiplier from x to y; same as the unit rate | 8 L in 10 h → k = 0.8 |
| y = kx | The equation form for proportional relationships | y = 2.54x (inches to cm) |
| Not y = kx | Anything with + or − a number, or x², or x in a denominator | y = 3x + 5, y = x + 1, y = 0.5x − 2 |

**Big idea:** k answers "y per 1 x." Once you have k, any question becomes y = k × x (find y) or x = y ÷ k (find x).

## Problem profile
| Type | Given → asked | Numbers | Format | Share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Find k | one pair, a table, or a picture → k | decimals (11.2 mi in 1.4 h), money, large numbers (600,000 gal/min) | typed | ~25% |
| B. Write y = kx | situation or table → the equation | k from ratios like 3 per 16, or "every dozen costs $15" (per item, k = 1.25) | write an equation, MC | ~25% |
| C. Use the equation | y = kx + new x or y → the missing value | e.g. 18 in → cm; find x when y = 408.5 | typed | ~20% |
| D. Proportional or not? | equation, table, or story (base fee, start height) → yes/no + explain | y = 3x + 5; admission + rides; a balloon starting at 5 ft | explain | ~20% |
| E. Two equations / units | same motion in minutes vs. hours; cheese per pizza → per slice | fractions | higher-order | ~10% |

**Traps:** "for every 3 lemons it's $1.80" means k = 0.60 per lemon, not 1.80. "A dozen for $15" means k = $1.25 per bagel. Multiple choice offers y = 15x and y = 1.25 + x as distractors. Watch which variable is x.

## Methods: ways to attack it

### Method 1: "k is y over x"
1. Name x (the input, the "per" thing) and y (the output).
2. k = y ÷ x using any one known pair (check a second pair if you have a table).
3. Write **y = (k)x**.
4. To find y: multiply. To find x: divide y by k.
**Memory hook:** "k is the key: y over x, then y = kx."
**Check it:** plug a known pair back in. Does y = k × x work?

### Method 2: Spot the "+" (proportional or not)
- An equation is proportional only if it is exactly **y = (number) × x**, with nothing added or subtracted.
- A story is proportional only if 0 of x gives 0 of y (no admission fee, no starting height).
**Memory hook:** "Any plus or minus? Not proportional, no fuss."

### Method 3: "Per 1" sentence
Say k as a sentence: "0.8 liters per 1 hour." If the sentence doesn't make sense, x and y are probably swapped.

## Guiding questions (homework help)
1. "What's x and what's y here? Which one depends on the other?"
2. "How much y for exactly 1 x?"
3. "Write it as y = __ x. What goes in the blank?"
4. "Are they asking for y or for x? Multiply or divide?"
5. For yes/no: "Is there a starting amount? What happens at zero?"

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | What it looks like | Nudge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| k = x/y | k = 1.25 instead of 0.8 | "Liters per hour: what goes on top?" |
| Uses the group price as k | y = 1.80x for "3 lemons for $1.80" | "Is $1.80 the price of ONE lemon?" |
| Calls y = x + 1 proportional | "It goes up steadily" | "Is it a times or a plus?" |
| Multiplies when she should divide | finding x from y | "You know y and k. What times k gives y?" |
| Mixes units | minutes in one equation, hours in another | "Is your k per minute or per hour?" |

## Variant recipes
- **A:** `Y units in X units`, with X a decimal (1.2..9.5) and k a "nice" decimal.
- **B:** `For every n items, cost rises $C` → k = C/n. Include MC distractors: y = Cx, y = C + x, x = ky.
- **C:** y = kx, given y (make it non-round) → find x.
- **D:** 4 options mixing y = kx, y = kx + b, y = x + b, and a story with a base fee.
- **E:** distance in fraction-of-a-minute time → write it per minute AND per hour (×60).

## Worked example (original)
*A printer makes 84 pages in 6 minutes at a constant rate. Write an equation and find the pages in 15 minutes.*
1. x = minutes, y = pages. k = 84 ÷ 6 = 14 pages per minute.
2. y = 14x.
3. y = 14(15) = 210 pages. Check: 6 min → 14 × 6 = 84. ✓
