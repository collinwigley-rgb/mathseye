# 2.2 — Determine Unit Rates with Ratios of Fractions

Source: Lesson 2-2 student pages, 2-2 Additional Practice (pp. 19–20) · Built: 2026-11-03

## In one sentence
Same "get to 1" move as 2.1, but now one or both amounts are fractions, so getting to 1 means **dividing fractions** (flip and multiply).

## Key concepts and vocabulary
| Term | Plain meaning | Tiny example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complex fraction | A fraction with a fraction on top and/or bottom | (2/3 mi) / (1/6 h) |
| Unit rate with fractions | Amount for exactly 1 of the "per" unit | 4 miles per hour |
| Reciprocal | The flipped fraction; multiplying by it makes 1 | 1/6 → 6, 3/4 → 4/3 |
| Unit fraction | A fraction with 1 on top | 1/4 hour = 15 minutes |
| Scale (maps, blueprints) | A map length matched to a real length | 3/4 in. : 3/5 mi |

**Big idea:** to turn the bottom into 1, multiply top AND bottom by the bottom's reciprocal. That's the same as dividing top ÷ bottom.

## Problem profile
| Type | Given → asked | Numbers | Format | Share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Unit rate from a complex fraction | (a/b) / (c/d) → unit rate | halves to twelfths; whole number over a fraction (7 mi in 1/4 h; 414 mi in 3/4 h) | typed, or a 2-column table | ~30% |
| B. Unit rate, then scale | rate in fractions + new amount → total | calories per 3/4 cup → 4 cups; area per 2/3 h → whole lawn | word problem | ~25% |
| C. Compare two or three rates | 2–3 people or products with fraction rates → fastest or cheapest | mixed numbers (2 1/2 mi in 3/5 h) | word problem | ~20% |
| D. Map and blueprint scale | map length : real distance, new map length → real (or reverse) | fractional inches or cm, miles or feet | word problem, diagram | ~15% |
| E. Two-way rate / critique | "tasks per hour" AND "hours per task"; spot a flipped rate | 8 tasks in 5/6 h | multi-part, explain | ~10% |

**Difficulty ladder:** a whole number over a unit fraction (7 ÷ 1/4) → a fraction over a fraction → mixed numbers → compare 3 rates → minutes to convert into hours (1 h 21 min) → unit conversion inside (square feet to square yards).

**Hidden conversions:** 15 min = 1/4 h, 20 min = 1/3 h, 1 h 21 min = 1 21/60 = 1 7/20 h, 9 sq ft = 1 sq yd.

## Methods: ways to attack it

### Method 1: "Flip the bottom" (divide fractions)
1. Write it as **top ÷ bottom**, with the "per" thing on the bottom.
2. Turn any mixed number into an improper fraction first.
3. Keep, change, flip: keep the top, change ÷ to ×, flip the bottom.
4. Cancel before you multiply, then simplify.
**Memory hook:** "Keep, Change, Flip, then Cancel before you Multiply."
**Check it:** dividing by a fraction less than 1 should make the answer *bigger*. 7 miles in 1/4 hour → more than 7 miles in a whole hour.

### Method 2: "How many pieces make 1?" (table)
```
Miles | 7   | 28
Hours | 1/4 | 1
        ×4  (four quarter-hours make an hour)
```
1. Ask: "How many of the bottom amount fit into 1?" (1/4 → 4; 1/3 → 3; 2/3 → 3/2)
2. Multiply both rows by that number.
**Best for:** unit fractions and friendly fractions. It's the same math as Method 1, shown as a picture.

### Method 3: Two steps, down then up
For awkward fractions like 2/3: first divide to get to a unit fraction (2/3 → 1/3, ÷2), then multiply up to 1 (×3).
Good for building number sense; the sheet shows this in the lawn-mowing example.

### Method 4: Minutes to hours first
If time is in minutes, rewrite it as a fraction of 60 before anything else: 15 min = 15/60 = 1/4 h.

## Guiding questions (homework help)
1. "What's the 'per' unit? That goes on the bottom."
2. "Any mixed numbers? What do they look like as improper fractions?"
3. "To get the bottom to exactly 1, what do you multiply it by?"
4. "Did you do the same thing to the top?"
5. "Should the answer be bigger or smaller than the top number? Why?"
6. For maps: "How many real miles does ONE inch stand for?"

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | What it looks like | Nudge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flips the wrong fraction | (2/3) ÷ (1/6) done as (3/2) × (1/6) | "Which one is the divisor? Only that one flips." |
| Upside-down rate | pounds per quart vs. quarts per pound (as in the critique problem) | "Read the label you want. What's on the bottom?" |
| Mixed number left mixed | 1 1/4 × 4 done as 1 × 4 + 1/4 | "Make it improper first." |
| Minutes used as whole numbers | 15 min treated as 15 hours | "How many minutes in an hour? So 15 min is what fraction?" |
| Answer smaller when it should be bigger | 7 ÷ 1/4 = 7/4 | "In a whole hour, do you go farther or less far than in a quarter hour?" |
| Skips the comparison step | finds 3 unit rates but doesn't order them | "Now line them up. Who's fastest?" |

## Variant recipes
- **A:** `(a/b) / (c/d)` with b, d in {2,3,4,5,6,8}, chosen so the answer is a whole number or a friendly mixed number. Easy: c = 1. Test-level: mixed numbers on both.
- **B:** `X units per (c/d) of Y; how many in N of Y?` N a whole number from 2 to 6.
- **C:** three people with distance in mixed miles and time in fraction hours; make the rates differ by at least 1/4 mph.
- **D:** `p/q inch = R miles on a map; a new map length m → real distance`, plus the reverse.
- **E:** `n tasks in c/d hour` → both the hours per task and the tasks per hour.

## Worked example (original)
*A snail-bot crawls 3/4 mile in 1/6 hour. What's its speed in miles per hour?*
1. Per hour, so hours go on the bottom: (3/4) ÷ (1/6).
2. Keep, change, flip: 3/4 × 6/1.
3. Cancel: 6 and 4 share a 2, so 3/2 × 3/1 = 9/2.
4. 9/2 = 4 1/2 miles per hour. Check: a quarter hour of travel is 3/4 mile, so a whole hour should be more. 4 1/2 is more. ✓
