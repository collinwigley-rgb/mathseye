# 2.5 — Graph Proportional Relationships

Source: Lesson 2-5 student pages, 2-5 Additional Practice (pp. 25–26) · Built: 2026-11-03

## In one sentence
A proportional relationship graphs as a **straight line through the origin (0, 0)**, and the point **(1, k)** shows the constant of proportionality.

## Key concepts and vocabulary
| Term | Plain meaning | Tiny example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origin | The point (0, 0) | 0 tickets cost $0 |
| Ordered pair (x, y) | A point on the graph: x across, y up | (1, 55) = 55 miles in 1 hour |
| (1, k) | The point where x = 1; its y is the unit rate | (1, 17) → $17 per pound |
| Straight line through (0, 0) | The test for proportional on a graph | y = 5x |
| Not proportional | A line that misses (0, 0), OR a curve through (0, 0) | a taxi with a starting fee |

**Big idea:** two checks, both required: (1) is it a straight line? (2) does it pass through (0, 0)? Any point (x, y) except the origin gives k = y/x.

## Problem profile
| Type | Given → asked | Numbers | Format | Share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Is the graph proportional? | 1–3 small graphs → yes/no + why | lines through the origin, lines that miss it, curves | explain | ~30% |
| B. Find k from a graph | a graph → k and what it means | read (1, k), or y/x of a labeled point; scales of 5, 25, 49, 55 | typed + meaning | ~25% |
| C. Interpret points | (0, 0), (1, k), (5, 275) → what each means in the story | | sentence | ~15% |
| D. Data → graph → decide | 3 data points (shells, tickets) → plot, decide, k | money | plot + explain | ~10% |
| E. Compare table vs. graph | Store A table vs. Store B graph → better deal + savings for N | money, exchange rates | multi-step | ~10% |
| F. Select all true | a graph + 5 statements (traps: swapped coordinates, "per") | | select-all | ~10% |

**Traps:** a statement like "(4, 68) means $4 buys 68 pounds" swaps x and y. "(1, 10) shows k" is wrong when the line doesn't actually pass through (1, 10). A line that starts at a fee ($3 shoe rental) is not proportional even though it's straight.

## Methods: ways to attack it

### Method 1: "Straight, and through zero"
1. Is it a straight line? (Use a ruler or a pencil edge.)
2. Does it pass through (0, 0)?
3. Both yes → proportional. Either no → not proportional, and say which check failed.
**Memory hook:** "Straight and through zero, or it's a no-go."

### Method 2: "Walk to x = 1"
Find where x = 1 and read y. That y is k. If x = 1 is hard to read, pick any clear point and compute y ÷ x.

### Method 3: Read the point as a sentence
For any (x, y), say: "x [x-label] gives y [y-label]." (1, 49) → "1 hour gives 49 miles." If the sentence sounds backwards, the statement swapped them.

### Method 4: Rise over run
From one point to the next: how much up for how much across? Up ÷ across = k (Tanya: +95 Calories per +10 minutes = 9.5).

## Guiding questions (homework help)
1. "Is it a straight line?"
2. "Where does it start? Does it touch (0, 0)?"
3. "What's y when x is 1?"
4. "Pick one point. Say it as a sentence with the labels."
5. For comparing: "Find each k. Is higher or lower better here?"

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | What it looks like | Nudge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Straight = proportional | calls the taxi line proportional | "Where does the line start?" |
| Swaps x and y | "(4, 68) means $4 for 68 lb" | "Which axis is pounds?" |
| Misreads the scale | counts gridlines as 1s when they're 5s | "What does one box stand for?" |
| k = x/y | 1/17 instead of 17 | "y over x: up value over across value." |
| Curve through the origin = proportional | | "Through zero, but is it straight?" |

## Variant recipes
- **A:** three mini graphs: y = kx, y = kx + b, and a curve (y = x²/4).
- **B:** a line through (0, 0) and (n, kn), with axis scales of 2, 5, 10, 25, 50; k whole or .5.
- **C:** a story graph with k = 18, 49, 55; ask about (0, 0), (1, k), (n, kn).
- **E:** Store A as a table (k1), Store B as a graph (k2), k differing by $0.05 to $0.50, buying 20 → savings.
- **F:** 5 statements including one with swapped coordinates and one with "per" misused.

## Worked example (original)
*A line passes through (0, 0) and (4, 30). Is it proportional? What's k?*
1. Straight line through (0, 0): yes, proportional.
2. k = 30 ÷ 4 = 7.5, so the point (1, 7.5) is on the line.
3. Equation: y = 7.5x. Check: (4, 30) → 7.5 × 4 = 30. ✓
