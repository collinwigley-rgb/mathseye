# 2.6 — Apply Proportional Reasoning to Solve Problems

Source: Lesson 2-6 student pages, 2-6 Additional Practice (pp. 27–28) · Built: 2026-11-03

## In one sentence
**First decide IF it's proportional** (a constant multiple?), then pick a tool (ratio table, bar diagram, graph, or y = kx) and solve.

## Key concepts and vocabulary
| Term | Plain meaning | Tiny example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constant multiple | y is always the same number × x | flour = 15/8 × milk |
| Constant difference (NOT proportional) | y is always x + some number | ages: Josh is always 4 years older |
| Inverse situation (NOT proportional) | more of one means less of the other | more people → less time to weed |
| Fixing a mix | adjust amounts so they match the ratio | 3 cherries : 8 grapes |
| Scale factor for area | lengths × k means area × k² | 2× wider and 2× taller → 4× the area |

**Big idea:** the trap problems in this lesson LOOK like ratio problems but aren't: ages, a start amount plus some per guest, people working together, or a noisy real-world table.

## Problem profile
| Type | Given → asked | Numbers | Format | Share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Can I use proportional reasoning? | a story → yes/no, then solve either way | ages, work crews, a "plus 1 pound per guest" | explain + solve | ~25% |
| B. Ratio stays the same when scaled | ratio a:b + one amount → the other; "what if both halve?" | 5:2 with 36; 3:7 with 14 | bar diagram, MC | ~20% |
| C. Use y = kx to check a claim | a rate + a bill or total → is it right? how far off? | $8.99/mo, a year charged as 16 months | word problem | ~15% |
| D. Fix a mixture / order | target ratio + wrong amounts → what to add or remove | 3:8 jar with 12 and 20 | explain | ~10% |
| E. Unit conversions inside | a table in inches, need feet/yards; minutes in hours | 24 ft = 288 in; 9 h 30 min = 9.5 h | multi-step | ~15% |
| F. Compare representations | a table vs. a graph (puppy weights in pounds vs. ounces) | unit mismatch | compare | ~10% |
| G. Scaling a group | 25 guests → 40 + 45 half-guests | mixed numbers | multi-step | ~5% |

## Methods: ways to attack it

### Method 1: "Times or plus?" (decide first)
1. Make a tiny table with 2–3 rows from the story.
2. Is y ÷ x the same each row? → **times** → proportional.
3. Is y − x the same each row (ages)? → **plus** → not proportional; just add.
4. Does one go down as the other goes up (workers vs. hours)? → not proportional.
**Memory hook:** "Times? Use a ratio. Plus? Just add. Flip? Think it through."

### Method 2: Pick the tool
| If you're given... | Reach for |
| --- | --- |
| a ratio plus one amount | bar diagram or ratio table |
| a rate plus a big total | y = kx, then solve for x or y |
| two sources to compare | k for each, same units |
| a mixture that's off | divide each amount by its ratio part, then match the bigger one |

### Method 3: Fix-the-mix
For a ratio 3 : 8 with 12 cherries and 20 grapes: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 groups, so you need 4 × 8 = 32 grapes (add 12). Or 20 ÷ 8 = 2.5 groups, so you need 7.5 cherries (remove 4.5, not possible with whole cherries, so add grapes).

### Method 4: Convert first
Get both quantities into the units the question asks for BEFORE using k (feet → inches, hours and minutes → decimal hours).

## Guiding questions (homework help)
1. "Make a quick table. Is it times or plus?"
2. "Does it make sense that doubling one doubles the other here?"
3. "Which tool fits best: table, bar, graph, or equation?"
4. "Are your units the same as what the question asks?"
5. "Now check: does your answer keep the same ratio?"

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | What it looks like | Nudge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uses ratios on ages | Hector 16 → Mary 6 | "In 8 years, how much older is Mary?" |
| Uses ratios on work crews | 6 people take 9 hours | "More helpers: more time or less?" |
| Ignores the start amount | chicken: 1 lb per guest, forgets the 8/9 lb | "Is there any chicken with zero guests?" |
| Mixes units | inch price × feet | "Is your rate per inch or per foot?" |
| Area scale = length scale | 2× sides → 2× area | "Draw it. How many original paintings fit?" |
| Answers only the yes/no | "Not proportional" and stops | "They also asked you to solve it." |

## Variant recipes
- **A:** ages (difference), crews (inverse), "base + per item" (not proportional), and a clean rate (proportional). Shuffle them.
- **B:** a ratio a:b with one total; then "both sell half" or "both buy N more" (the ratio changes!).
- **C:** a monthly rate × 12 vs. a billed amount; find the overcharge in dollars and in months.
- **D:** a ratio p:q, a jar with mismatched counts → "add how many of which?"
- **E:** a price-per-inch table → cost of F feet or Y yards.

## Worked example (original)
*A smoothie uses 2 cups of berries for every 3 cups of yogurt. You have 9 cups of yogurt. How many cups of berries?*
1. Times or plus? Double the recipe and both double, so it's times: proportional.
2. 9 ÷ 3 = 3 batches. 3 × 2 = 6 cups of berries.
3. Check: 6 : 9 simplifies to 2 : 3. ✓
